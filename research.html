Research

My research focuses on solar physics, particularly the theoretical description and numerical modeling of polarized radiation in the solar atmosphere. I am interested in how the polarization of spectral lines can provide information about the physical conditions of the solar atmosphere and its magnetic fields.

Polarized Radiative Transfer in the Solar Atmosphere

The radiation emerging from the solar atmosphere carries information about the physical processes taking place within it. In addition to its intensity, the polarization of radiation provides important diagnostic information about the properties of the emitting and scattering material.

I am interested in the theoretical foundations of polarized radiative transfer, including the description of radiation through the Stokes parameters and the relationship between radiation anisotropy, atomic polarization, and emergent spectral line polarization.

The Hanle Effect and Solar Magnetic Fields

The Hanle effect describes the modification of scattering polarization by magnetic fields. It provides a diagnostic tool for investigating magnetic fields in the solar atmosphere through their influence on the polarization of spectral lines.

My interests include the theoretical description of the Hanle effect, the dependence of polarization on magnetic-field strength and orientation, and the calculation and interpretation of polarized signals for different observing geometries.

Numerical Modeling of Polarized Radiation

Numerical modeling provides a way to investigate the theoretical relationships between radiation fields, atomic polarization, magnetic fields, and observable spectral line signals.

My computational work uses Python to implement and test theoretical formulations of polarized radiation, examine polarization in the presence of magnetic fields, and reproduce analytical results and reference calculations. This provides a foundation for further development of models of polarized radiative transfer in the solar atmosphere.
